Higgs Audio v3 TTS#

Higgs Audio v3 TTS (bosonai/higgs-tts-3-4b): a Qwen3-4B backbone over text tokens and the 8 delayed codebooks of the Higgs audio tokenizer (25 frames/s, 24 kHz).

vox-serve --model bosonai/higgs-tts-3-4b --port 8000
curl -X POST localhost:8000/generate -F "text=Hello world" -F "streaming=true" -o out.wav

# Voice cloning: reference audio and its transcript
curl -X POST localhost:8000/generate -F "text=Hello world" -F "audio=@ref.wav" \
  -F "ref_text=Transcript of the reference clip." -o out.wav

Voices used on every request can stay on the server: with --voice-dir voices/, holding guide.wav and its transcript guide.txt, a request picks the voice with voice=guide (also the voice of a WebSocket session.config) and uploads nothing. ref_audio_path=sub/clip.wav names any clip under the directory. The reference is prefilled on every request: about 70 ms of first-audio latency for a 17 s clip on Jetson Thor, so a shorter clip starts faster.

Inline control tags (<|emotion:elation|>, <|prosody:pause|>, <|sfx:laughter|>, …) go in the text; see the model’s PROMPTING.md.

Input Streaming#

Text can be sent while audio is generated (e.g. from a text LLM), through the same endpoints as other models (/generate/stream/start, .../{id}/text, .../{id}/audio, .../{id}/end; see the example client). It needs the Rust runtime, and no scheduler flag.

Higgs reads text in turns: the model’s multi-turn layout <|text|> chunk <|audio|> [audio] <|text|> chunk <|audio|> [audio] ... in one sequence, so the voice and prosody carry over and earlier turns stay in the context. A turn ends at a clause boundary (. , ! ? ; :): the first one starts at the first boundary, and each later turn takes the text that arrived while the previous turn’s audio was generated, up to its last boundary. A turn boundary costs a short pause, like a comma. Only a run-on sentence of 20 or more words without punctuation is cut at a word boundary, because a turn that ends mid-phrase can make the model add or swallow a word there.

When the text arrives more slowly than it is spoken, the audio waits for it: the current turn plays to its end, and the next one starts when its clause is complete. Send text with its punctuation and spaces as they arrive.

The same streaming is available over WebSocket at /v1/audio/speech/stream, with sglang-omni’s protocol (session.config, input.text, input.commit, input.done; see the API reference and the example client). Each commit starts a new request, so a long conversation does not fill the context, and split_granularity: "sentence" makes every turn a whole sentence.

Every turn stays in the KV cache: a stream ends (max_context_reached) once text and audio fill --max-context (2048 rows by default, about 70 seconds of speech). Raise it for long streams; the model was trained with 8192.
